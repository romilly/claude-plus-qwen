Nine Men's Morris

About the Game

Nine Men's Morris is an ancient strategy board game dating back to around 100 AD, when it was played in the Roman Empire. Two players compete by placing and moving their pieces to form “mills” (three in a row), which allows them to remove an opponent’s piece.

The Board

The board consists of three concentric squares connected by lines at their midpoints. There are 24 intersection points where pieces can be placed.

Phases of Play

  1. Placing Phase — Players alternate placing their 9 pieces on any empty point on the board. Try to form mills while setting up future threats.
  2. Moving Phase — Once all 18 pieces are placed, players move one piece at a time along the board lines to an adjacent empty point.
  3. Flying Phase — When a player is down to only 3 pieces, they can “fly”: any of their remaining pieces can jump to ANY empty point on the board.

Forming Mills

When you get three of your pieces in a row along a line, you form a mill. You may then remove one of your opponent’s pieces from the board. Pieces that are part of a mill cannot be removed unless ALL of the opponent’s pieces are already in mills.

Winning

  • Reduce your opponent to only 2 pieces, OR
  • Block your opponent so they have no legal moves

Strategy Tips

  • Build double mills: Set up configs where one move completes two mills — opponent can only remove one.
  • Block opponent mills: Place pieces to prevent your opponent from forming mills.
  • Control the center: Inner points have more connectivity options.
  • Save flying for later: Keep as many pieces on the board as possible.

Controls

  • Green glow — valid place point (placing phase)
  • Blue glow — valid move destination (moving phase)
  • Purple glow — any empty point (flying phase)
  • Gold ring — your selected piece
  • Red pulse — removable opponent piece
